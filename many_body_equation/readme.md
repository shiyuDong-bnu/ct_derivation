Merging rules, as implemented in `expand_commutators.py` (`canonicalize`,
orbit minimum): two terms are equal -- and merge -- iff they are related
by identities that are exact in the spin-integrated, real-coefficient
convention:
a. renaming of any summed index within its space.  ALL indices are
   summed: the free indices of the operator are the summation labels of
   the operator basis sum, so they can be renamed like tensor dummies
   (this subsumes the old letter-substitution rules a--d);
b. the pair symmetry of the amplitudes, t^{ab}_{ij} = t^{ba}_{ji} and
   g^{pq}_{rs} = g^{qp}_{sr} (simultaneous swap of both index pairs) --
   no index antisymmetry of t or g is used anywhere;
c. real coefficients: f symmetric (f^q_p = f^p_q) and g symmetric under
   pair exchange (g^{pq}_{rs} = g^{rs}_{pq}); the density matrices D
   (appearing in the cumulant stage) carry the same symmetries;
d. the exact fermionic row antisymmetry of the E operator, the parity
   sign going into the coefficient;
e. merge all equal operators (the canonical form is the minimum over the
   orbit generated by a--d; merged coefficients carry the orbit sign).
